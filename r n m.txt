Key Credit Risks & Mitigants

1. Elevated exposure to equities and sensitivity to market volatility

Risk:
MS&AD maintains a relatively high exposure to equities compared with similarly rated global peers, making capital and earnings more sensitive to fluctuations in Japanese stock prices. Strategic shareholdings have historically contributed to volatility in capital assessments and have been cited by regulators as a contributing factor to pricing issues among large P&C insurers.

Mitigants:
The group is actively reducing its equity holdings, with a plan to divest strategic stocks completely by fiscal 2029. Recent decreases in domestic equity exposure have supported ESR stability, while MS&AD’s high-quality investment portfolio and strong liquidity help to cushion market-driven capital movements.


---

2. Significant natural catastrophe (NatCat) exposure driving earnings volatility

Risk:
MS&AD faces high physical catastrophe risk in Japan (typhoons, floods, earthquakes), which contributes to volatile profitability in domestic P&C segments. MS Amlin’s specialty and Lloyd’s businesses also bring material NatCat and tail-risk exposure, increasing the volatility of group earnings and reserving outcomes.

Mitigants:
The group has comprehensive reinsurance arrangements that help temper the impact of large catastrophe losses. Geographic diversification has improved, with overseas premium income rising to around 30% of NPW, helping reduce reliance on Japan-only exposures. Strong capitalization—including a 1.6x gross underwriting leverage and an ESR above 230%—provides additional resilience against NatCat shocks.


---

3. Pressure on domestic P&C profitability, especially auto and fire lines

Risk:
Domestic auto insurance profitability remains weak due to higher accident frequencies, persistent inflation in repair costs, and elevated accident claims. Fire insurance performance has also been pressured by the increasing severity and frequency of natural disasters. Combined, these trends have led to strained earnings in core domestic P&C operations.

Mitigants:
The group has raised premium rates for both auto and fire insurance, with further adjustments implemented in January 2024 and 2025. These rate actions, combined with operational efficiency programs and improving overseas contributions, are expected to support gradual profitability recovery over the next 12–18 months.


---

4. Higher tail-risk profile and governance complexity in overseas operations (MS Amlin)

Risk:
Although improving, MS Amlin’s specialty and Lloyd’s portfolios carry higher-than-domestic risk of adverse reserve development, business interruption losses, and event-cancellation exposures. Historical governance issues at MS AUL, including PRA sanctions through 2019, highlight the operational challenges of managing overseas entities within the group.

Mitigants:
MS Amlin has returned to profitability following several loss-making years, supported by premium rate increases, portfolio clean-up actions, better reserving, and improved risk management processes. These improvements have reduced earnings drag and strengthened the overall stability of the group’s overseas business.


---

5. External constraints on capital assessments due to linkage with Japan sovereign rating

Risk:
The financial strength ratings of ADI and MSI are capped by the rating of Japanese government bonds (JGBs). Any deterioration in sovereign credit quality would directly constrain MS&AD’s IFSR, regardless of the group’s intrinsic capitalization or earnings improvements.

Mitigants:
Despite the sovereign cap, MS&AD maintains very strong capitalization, with stable ESR levels and a track record of prudent capital management. Strong earnings coverage, low leverage at the holding-company level, and ample liquidity provide buffers against external rating constraints.
